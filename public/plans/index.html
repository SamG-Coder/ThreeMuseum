Your Blender exhibit schedule

Dimensions are width × height × depth. Each origin is bottom-centred. These are installation allowances, not claims about animal size.

D01 · SPLIT

Mamenchisaurus

7 × 8.5 × 28 m

A long, largely horizontal sauropod mount. Two separated support islands leave a 7 m cross-passage through the reserved bay. Keep the passage clear above 2.8 m; validate the final neck, tail and supports before installation.

D02 · PLINTH

Tarbosaurus

5 × 5.8 × 11 m

A tall theropod mount, read from the connecting walk and the north overlook. Orient the long axis along Z. Reserve the full tail sweep inside the envelope.

D03 · PLINTH

Armoured dinosaur

5.6 × 3.5 × 5 m

Armour, tail mechanics and defensive body plans. No moving parts are supplied; use a separate animation clip later.

D04 · PLINTH

Protoceratops

3.2 × 2.5 × 3.2 m

A family-scale mount with a lower reading rail. Its envelope intentionally leaves the dinosaur entrance unobstructed.

D05 · TABLE

Trackway casting

3 × 0.3 × 2 m

A shallow, accessible examination surface for a trackway cast. The display table is already built; add only the geological exhibit.

D06 · WALL

Comparative teeth

0.45 × 1.1 × 2.6 m

A side-facing cabinet above the ramp. Compare feeding adaptations using original tooth models. Keep all geometry inside the shallow case.

D07 · PLINTH

Australian megafauna

4 × 3.6 × 4 m

Reserve for a Diprotodon or another Australian megafaunal subject. Label its different time period explicitly; do not call every prehistoric animal a dinosaur.

D08 · SUSPENDED

Pterosaur

7 × 2.8 × 4 m

Suspended pterosaur allowance. It is not a dinosaur. Model rigging separately, check supports against roof ribs, and keep the entire envelope above visitor head height.

D09 · TABLE

Fossil comparison

4 × 0.45 × 1.25 m

An overlook-level comparison table. The shell includes the table and guardrails; add fossil specimens only.

B01 · WALL

Butterfly diversity

3 × 1.8 × 0.38 m

Pinned-specimen composition in a shallow wall cabinet. Use separate small models, or an original baked specimen atlas for distant views. No specimen artwork is supplied.

B02 · WALL

Beetle diversity

5.4 × 1.8 × 0.38 m

A second framed collection wall with warm conservation-style presentation. Design allowances are not real conservation specifications.

B03 · CASE

Insect Hall of Fame theme

3 × 1.65 × 2 m

An island vitrine for insect superlatives. Confirm any eventual largest/heaviest/longest claim with a species-specific source before adding final labels.

B04 · TABLE

Insect classification

3 × 0.4 × 1.25 m

Low comparison table for body plan models. Deliberately separate insect classification from the eight-legged gallery next door.

B05 · HABITAT

Green tree ant colony

3.5 × 2.4 × 3 m

Empty habitat enclosure. You will supply nest, branches, substrate, ant meshes and animation. This is not a live-animal husbandry design.

B06 · HABITAT

Mantid habitat

1.35 × 1.8 × 2.6 m

Wall-side enclosure reserved for mantid, branches and foliage. No placeholder insects or greenery are included.

B07 · HABITAT

Aquatic invertebrates

1.35 × 1.8 × 2.6 m

An aquarium-format shell with a dry, empty interior. Add water and invertebrates later; avoid nested transmissive surfaces in the final GLB.

B08 · HABITAT

Decomposer habitat

3 × 1.8 × 1.25 m

An empty ecological exhibit for leaf litter and decomposer invertebrates. Habitat dressing remains part of your Blender stage.

B09 · PLINTH

Insect body systems

4 × 2.9 × 4 m

A deliberately enlarged anatomical model. Display its scale multiplier on the future label so visitors do not confuse model size with animal size.

B10 · CASE

Insect mouthparts

2.8 × 1.1 × 1.6 m

Enlarged comparative mouthparts inside an island vitrine. Include chewing, piercing or other forms only after choosing referenced species.

B11 · WALL

Life cycles

5.8 × 1.8 × 0.38 m

A chronological specimen sequence. The cabinet and typography frame are present; model the lifecycle stages in Blender.

B12 · TABLE

Pollination

2.7 × 0.7 × 1.5 m

A teaching-table allowance for insect–flower relationships. Flowers and insects are both intentionally omitted.

B13 · CASE

Arachnid comparison

3 × 1.35 × 2 m

A comparison vitrine introducing arachnids. Use accurate leg counts and label specimens independently from insects.

B14 · HABITAT

Tarantula enclosure

1.35 × 1.8 × 2.6 m

An empty quarantine-themed enclosure. No fictional biosecurity system is presented as a working or legally compliant facility.

B15 · HABITAT

Local spider enclosure

1.35 × 1.8 × 2.6 m

An enclosure for a chosen locally occurring spider. Final species, location and safety interpretation must be researched before publication.

B16 · WALL

Web structures

4.6 × 1.8 × 0.38 m

A backlit web-study cabinet. Supply the silk geometry or baked transparent texture; keep the future exhibit readable without bloom.

F01 · PLINTH

Triceratops

6.5 × 5 × 11 m

The feature-gallery mount with a full walk-around route. Inspired by the idea of Melbourne’s Triceratops gallery; this is not a model or reproduction of Horridus.

F02 · WALL

Fossil preparation

5 × 1.8 × 0.38 m

A wall-case allowance for preparation tools, rock matrix and fossil evidence. All these assets are left for Blender.

L01 · TABLE

Handling collection

4 × 0.55 × 2 m

A school-group table for tactile or digital models. The runtime interaction is a readable design card, not a finished scientific activity.

L02 · TABLE

Microscope station

2 × 0.8 × 1.3 m

Reserve for microscope and specimen-tray assets. Do not export room furniture with the specimen GLB.

C01 · PLANTER

Botanical installation

2.2 × 6 × 12 m

An empty central planting bed. Trees, ferns, ground cover and fauna are excluded from this delivery. This is a quiet light-court interpretation, not the real Forest Gallery.
